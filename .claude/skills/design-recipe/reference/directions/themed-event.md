# Direction dossier: Themed event

> A small sub-brand world, grown from the event's own truth, framed by Esportra's invariants.

Some events deserve their own identity: a Ramadan night cup, a women's circuit, a university league, a charity cup with a children's hospital, a retro fighting-game night, a city championship. Forcing them into the core Broadcast look makes them feel generic; letting them float free makes them feel unofficial. Themed event is the middle path.

---

## 1. When

S7 latitude L3; the event has a real identity worth expressing (its cause, city, community, era, season); it lives on event-owned surfaces (key art, social, venue, event landing page). **Product surfaces keep the core system**; the event appears there only as its name, crest/mark and possibly a thin accent rule on the event page header.

## 2. How to build the world (in this order)

1. **Find the theme's truth.** Interview (per `discovery-first`): what is this event *about* for the people in it? A charity cup is about who it helps; a women's circuit is about its players' own identity (ask them - don't impose pink); a Ramadan night cup is about late nights, community, iftar before the first map.
2. **Choose 3-4 variables to move** (from `esportra-brand` §4): palette temperature and one event accent, a display typeface pairing, a motif (pattern, shape, texture), imagery style. Keep the rest.
3. **Keep the invariants:** honest words, one hero, one cue per composition (the event accent may replace rose *as* the cue on event surfaces - never both), player-first, accessibility, exact time and money.
4. **Lock up with Esportra:** a consistent "on Esportra" / "hosted on Esportra" mark in mono at the event's footer and on its bracket.
5. **Write a mini style sheet** in `creative/theme-<event>.md`: palette (4-6 named hex), type pairing, motif rules, two do/don'ts, sample lockups. Get it approved before production.

## 3. Guardrails

- The theme comes from the event's subject, not from a trend.
- Never use culturally or religiously significant motifs decoratively without the community's input.
- Contrast and legibility rules do not relax.
- The theme ends with the event; it doesn't leak into the core product.

## 4. Worked example: charity cup for a children's hospital

Truth: players want their game night to matter. Idea: "Every match funds a bed." Moves: warm Daylight ground on social and posters (the hospital's own teal as the event accent, replacing rose as the cue), a friendly rounded display pairing approved for the event only, the hospital's name and logo co-branded (in partnership with). Hero: the running total raised, as caption-over-number. Product: the tournament page header gets a thin teal rule and the charity mark; everything else stays core.
