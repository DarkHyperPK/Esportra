# Creative brief and hand-off contract

A junior agent is only as good as its brief. A thin brief produces the default look; a sharp brief produces work that feels inevitable. The rule runs both ways: whoever receives a thin brief must ask (per `discovery-first`), not guess.

## The brief

Copy, fill in, hand over. **A brief with empty fields is a question list, not a brief.**

```
CREATIVE BRIEF - [piece name]                    Project: PROJ-XXX   Owner: [agent]

MOMENT        Where on the arc (anticipation / nerves / match / outcome / belonging / trouble)
AUDIENCE      Who exactly, in one line (role, place, experience level, device)
TRUTH         What they feel or fear right now, in their words
IDEA          One sentence, no "and"
HERO          The one element that carries the idea (number / name / face / date / state)
SIGNALS       S1 surface · S2 mode · S3 arc · S4 audience · S5 density · S6 emotion · S7 latitude · S8 constraints
DIRECTION     Proposed lead direction (+ blend) - or "open: propose 2-3 routes"
ARCHETYPE     Stage / face-off / scoreboard / path / spotlight / fork / verdict / pulse
MANDATORIES   Logos, legal lines, partners, dates, prices, exact names and spellings
FORMATS       Sizes and media (16:9 / 4:5 / 9:16 / product / push / email / print)
WORDS         Headline, caption, action drafts - or "propose three"
AVOID         Specific traps for this piece
SUCCESS       What the audience does or feels afterwards; how we will know
DELIVERABLES  Files, variants, states, deadline
ANSWERS       Links to recorded clarifications that shaped this brief
```

## The hand-off contract

What the maker returns, in this order:

1. **Understanding** - the brief restated in their own words, plus every assumption made.
2. **Open questions** - each tagged BLOCKING / SHAPING, with the default used if unanswered.
3. **Two or three routes** - each a different honest answer (different direction, archetype or hero - never colour variants), each with a one-line rationale tied to the truth.
4. **Recommendation** - one route, and why, referencing the signals.
5. **Direction Contract** - for the recommended route (template in `direction-engine.md`).
6. **Rubric** - scored for the recommended route, with evidence per line.
7. **Final files** - in every format and state requested, only after a route is chosen.

## The review loop

- Feedback names the rubric question it relates to ("Q3: two heroes are fighting") so the fix goes to the cause.
- A route that fails questions 1-4 goes back to the brief, not to polishing.
- Two rounds of revision on the same point means the brief was unclear: fix the brief.

## The recipe in one breath

> Find the moment and the truth. Say one idea in one sentence. Give it one hero. Read the room and choose the direction that fits - not the one you used last time. Choose the shape of attention, dress it with our ingredients: honest words, one cue of light, two voices of type, the moves that make it ours. Taste it with the rubric. Remove one more thing. Ship it, proud but never loud.
